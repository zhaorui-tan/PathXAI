Specimen/Site:
Adrenal gland / adrenocortical tissue

Diagnosis:
Adrenocortical carcinoma

Tumor/Lesion Description:
The submitted tissue sections demonstrate a neoplastic proliferation of large polygonal cells with abundant eosinophilic to clear vacuolated cytoplasm, consistent with adrenocortical differentiation. The tumor cells are arranged in diffuse sheets and solid nests. Segmentation analysis confirms a high neoplastic burden (up to 98.2% in sampled ROIs) with minimal intervening stroma.

Key Microscopic Findings:
The neoplastic cells exhibit significant cytologic atypia, characterized by marked nuclear pleomorphism, hyperchromasia, and prominent nucleoli. The chromatin pattern is coarse and irregular. The architectural pattern is diffuse and solid, lacking the organized zonation of normal adrenal cortex. Stromal inflammation is minimal.

Invasion/Necrosis/Margins:
Extensive geographic necrosis is present within the tumor parenchyma (segmentation "Dead" class up to 13.7%). Definitive evidence of capsular invasion, vascular invasion, or surgical margin status is not assessable from H&E ROI evidence.

Immunohistochemistry/Ancillary Studies:
Not assessable from H&E ROI evidence. Recommended stains to confirm adrenocortical lineage and exclude metastasis include Inhibin-alpha, Melan-A, SF-1, and Synaptophysin.

Stage/Grade/Prognostic Features:
Malignancy: malignant based on the final diagnosis; exact grade, Weiss score, stage, and prognostic risk are not assessable from current H&E ROI evidence.

Final Summary:
The morphologic features are diagnostic of Adrenocortical carcinoma. The differential diagnoses of metastatic renal cell carcinoma and metastatic melanoma are considered but less likely given the specific cytologic features; however, immunohistochemistry is required for definitive exclusion. Clinical correlation regarding tumor size and hormonal activity is recommended.

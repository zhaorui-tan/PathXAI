Tumor/Lesion Description:
The submitted tissue sections demonstrate a neoplastic proliferation consistent with adrenocortical origin. The tumor is composed of solid sheets and nests of large polygonal cells with abundant eosinophilic to clear (lipidized) cytoplasm. The neoplastic cells exhibit marked nuclear pleomorphism, hyperchromasia, and prominent nucleoli. Segmentation analysis indicates a high tumor burden across the sampled regions (Neoplastic fraction up to 97.0%).

- **Nuclear Features:** Nuclei are enlarged and pleomorphic with prominent nucleoli.
- **Stroma:** Delicate fibrovascular septa separate the nests of tumor cells. Stromal inflammation is minimal.

Final Summary:
The histologic features are consistent with Adrenocortical Carcinoma. The tumor is composed of atypical adrenocortical cells with necrosis. While the morphology supports a malignant diagnosis, the absence of a complete Weiss score assessment (specifically mitotic count and vascular invasion) and lack of immunohistochemical confirmation limits the precision of the grading. Differential diagnoses such as metastatic renal cell carcinoma or hepatocellular carcinoma should be excluded with appropriate clinical correlation and immunohistochemistry.

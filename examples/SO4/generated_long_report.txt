Specimen/Site:
Adrenal gland / adrenocortical tissue

Diagnosis:
Adrenocortical carcinoma

Tumor/Lesion Description:
The submitted tissue sections demonstrate a neoplastic proliferation of adrenocortical origin. The tumor is composed of diffuse sheets and nests of large polygonal cells with abundant eosinophilic to vacuolated (lipid-rich) cytoplasm. The neoplastic cells exhibit marked nuclear pleomorphism, hyperchromasia, and prominent nucleoli. Segmentation analysis confirms a high neoplastic cell fraction (up to 98.2% in sampled ROIs) with minimal interspersed connective tissue stroma.

Key Microscopic Findings:
Microscopic examination reveals a high-grade neoplasm characterized by diffuse sheets of atypical adrenocortical cells. The cells display enlarged, irregular nuclei with prominent nucleoli. The tumor architecture is disorganized, with cells arranged in solid nests and trabeculae. There is a distinct lack of the organized zonation seen in normal adrenal cortex.

Invasion/Necrosis/Margins:
Necrosis: Extensive geographic necrosis is present, characterized by large areas of eosinophilic cellular debris and loss of nuclear detail.
Invasion: Assessment of capsular invasion, vascular invasion, and invasion into adjacent normal adrenal tissue is not assessable from H&E ROI evidence.
Margins: Surgical margins are not assessable from H&E ROI evidence.

Final Summary:
The morphologic features are consistent with a high-grade Adrenocortical carcinoma. The diagnosis is supported by the presence of extensive geographic necrosis and marked cytologic atypia within an adrenocortical neoplasm. Differential diagnoses such as metastatic renal cell carcinoma, atypical adrenocortical adenoma, and pheochromocytoma are considered; however, the extent of necrosis and high-grade features favor carcinoma. Confirmation of lineage and exclusion of metastasis would require immunohistochemical staining (e.g., Melan-A, SF-1, Inhibin-alpha) and clinical correlation.

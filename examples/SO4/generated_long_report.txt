adrenocortical carcinoma

Tumor/Lesion Description:
The submitted tissue sections demonstrate a neoplastic proliferation of adrenocortical origin. The tumor is composed of diffuse sheets and nests of large polygonal cells with abundant eosinophilic to vacuolated (lipid-rich) cytoplasm. The neoplastic cells exhibit marked nuclear pleomorphism, hyperchromasia, and prominent nucleoli. The tumor architecture is solid, lacking the organized zonation of normal adrenal cortex.

Key Microscopic Findings:
Microscopic examination reveals a high-grade neoplasm characterized by significant cytologic atypia. The tumor cells display irregular nuclear contours and prominent nucleoli. There is evidence of extensive geographic necrosis, characterized by large areas of eosinophilic debris and loss of cellular detail, surrounded by viable tumor cells. Segmentation analysis supports a high neoplastic burden (up to 98.2% in sampled ROIs) with associated necrotic tissue. Hemorrhage is present within the tumor stroma.

Final Summary:
The morphologic features are diagnostic of a high-grade adrenocortical carcinoma. The diagnosis is supported by the presence of marked cytologic atypia, extensive geographic necrosis, and a solid growth pattern of lipid-rich cells. Differential diagnoses such as metastatic renal cell carcinoma and metastatic melanoma are considered but are less likely given the specific cytologic features; however, immunohistochemistry is recommended for definitive confirmation. Adrenocortical adenoma is excluded based on the high-grade features and necrosis.
